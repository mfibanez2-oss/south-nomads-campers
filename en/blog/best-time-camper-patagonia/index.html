The best time to travel Patagonia by camper: month by month

"When's the best time to go?" has three different answers depending on what you're optimising for: weather, price or solitude. You never get all three. Here's the month-by-month comparison, with the good and the bad of each window.

This question arrives in every email, and the honest answer starts with another question: what matters most to you — weather, price, or not meeting anyone? Because in Patagonia those three things fight each other.

The three-line summary

  • Most stable weather, everything running: January and February. Also the most expensive and the busiest.
  • My favourite window: November, and March into the first half of April. Colour, quiet, lower prices, variable but manageable weather.
  • Lowest price: May to August, with less daylight and harder logistics.

The three seasons and what they cost

Our rates follow exactly that logic:

  • High season (November to March): Nomads M CLP 193,000/day, Nomads L CLP 219,000/day.
  • Shoulder season (April, September, October): Nomads M CLP 158,000/day, Nomads L CLP 180,000/day.
  • Low season (May to August): Nomads M CLP 134,000/day, Nomads L CLP 152,000/day.

A 14-day trip in April costs considerably less than the same trip in January — and in April the forest is red. If your work lets you choose, that's the move.

And remember the 15-day threshold: past it the rate drops retroactively across the whole rental. The numbers are in the camper rental guide.

Wind, the variable nobody budgets for

People plan around rain and then discover the problem is wind. In spring and early summer, particularly toward Magallanes and Torres del Paine, it can blow hard and constantly for days.

What that means in practice with a camper:

  • More fuel burned and more fatigue at the wheel.
  • Cooking outside becomes impossible; inside, nothing changes.
  • Parking nose into the wind is the difference between sleeping well and listening to the camper all night.
  • Ferries get cancelled for wind and swell more often than you'd expect.

Autumn and winter are noticeably calmer. It's one of the reasons I like April so much.

Camper travel in winter: does it make sense?

Yes, conditionally. Cold isn't the problem: the diesel heater runs independently of the engine and keeps the camper comfortable all night. What changes is everything else:

  • Daylight: in June you're driving in daylight roughly between nine and half past five. Days get planned differently.
  • Morning ice: frosted gravel means starting later and driving slower.
  • Ferries and services: fewer departures, and some campsites and restaurants closed.
  • Border passes: can close with snow. Another reason not to plan long crossings in winter.

My winter recommendation: stay in the lakes and volcanoes region, with the option of dropping to Chaitén if the weather holds. It's a different Patagonia — snow on the volcanoes and not a single queue.

What I'd choose

  1. First time, long route, want certainty: second half of December, or February.
  2. Best value for money: March or November.
  3. Autumn photographs and silence: April, with a shorter, more flexible itinerary.
  4. Tight budget, short trip: May to August around Puerto Varas and the lakes.

Whichever month you pick, leave a spare day before your flight home. Patagonian weather always collects something in the end, and that extra day is what keeps the bill small.

Frequently asked

What's the best time to drive the Carretera Austral?

December to March if you want maximum certainty that everything is running and the days are long. November and March-April if you'd rather have fewer people and better prices, accepting more variable weather.

Can you travel Patagonia by camper in winter?

Yes, with care and a shorter itinerary. Our campers have diesel heaters, so cold is manageable. What changes is the logistics: less daylight, ice in the mornings and reduced ferry frequency.

When is Patagonia least windy?

Generally autumn and winter are calmer than spring. The windiest months tend to be October through January, especially around Torres del Paine and Magallanes.

When are camper rental prices lowest in Chile?

May to August is low season, with rates around 30-40% below January. April, September and October are shoulder season, a good balance of price and weather.
