Puerto Varas by camper: seven trips less than two hours away

Most people arrive in Puerto Varas treating it as the starting line for the Carretera Austral. We live here, and the truth is that a week and a camper barely scratches what sits within two hours of town.

By Rodrigo Published August 6, 2026 · 10 min read

We live in Puerto Varas and build the campers here. When we hand one over, the customer is usually already looking south toward the Carretera Austral. Fair enough. But every season someone comes back early because of weather or a change of plan, and discovers that the lake district had a whole trip in it.

Why Puerto Varas works as a basecamp

Three practical reasons, beyond the scenery:

  • It's 20 minutes from Puerto Montt airport, which has daily flights from Santiago. Land in the morning and you're driving the camper after lunch.
  • It has big supermarkets, hardware stores and workshops. It's the last cheap place to shop before heading south, and the best place to solve anything you're missing.
  • Everything good is close. Volcanoes, ancient native forest, fjords, hot springs and an entire island, all within two hours' drive.

Seven trips less than two hours away

1. Osorno volcano and Lake Llanquihue (1 hour)

The road climbs to the ski centre and the viewpoints. On a clear day you see Calbuco, Puntiagudo and the whole lake. A camper gets up there without trouble and the parking is generous. Tip: go early — afternoon cloud covers the cone with almost offensive regularity.

2. Petrohué falls and Lake Todos los Santos (1 hour)

Inside Vicente Pérez Rosales, Chile's oldest national park. Turquoise water cutting through volcanic rock, exactly like the photos. Carry on to the Petrohué jetty for the emerald lake with Osorno behind it.

3. Cochamó and the Reloncaví estuary (1 hour 45)

My personal favourite. A valley of granite walls people call the Chilean Yosemite, reached on narrow gravel roads where a 4x4 camper earns its keep. The trek into the valley is long and demanding, but the estuary, the timber houses and the curanto cooked in the ground are worth the drive even if you never walk a step.

4. Alerce Andino National Park (1 hour)

Alerce trees over three thousand years old, wet green trails, and far fewer people than Petrohué. The access roads are gravel and get muddy in winter — exactly the kind of detour where 4x4 stops being an extra.

5. Frutillar and the north shore (45 min)

German settler architecture, a concert hall on the lake, and real kuchen. This is the easy-day option, ideal for day one while you settle into the camper.

6. Chiloé (2 hours, including the ferry)

The ferry from Pargua to Chacao is short and frequent. On the far side: UNESCO wooden churches, the stilt houses of Castro, Chiloé National Park with huge beaches at Cucao, and seafront campsites. Two or three nights vanish quickly.

7. Hot springs: Puyehue, Ralún and the smaller ones (1-2 hours)

There are big resort springs and small half-hidden ones. After three days in a camper, an afternoon in hot water is the closest thing to luxury this region sells.

Practicalities: shopping, fuel and campsites

  • Big supermarkets: in Puerto Varas and Puerto Montt. Buy the whole trip here if you're heading south; prices climb with every kilometre.
  • Fuel: plentiful across the region. The last comfortable fill before the deep gravel is Puerto Montt or Hornopirén.
  • Camping gear: shops in Puerto Varas and in the Puerto Montt mall, including Decathlon — which is where we send people for the microfibre towels we don't include.
  • Campsites: Ensenada and the volcano road have several good ones. In high season, arrive early in the afternoon.
  • Mobile signal: good across the region, except inland from Cochamó and in parts of Alerce Andino.

The weather here, without the romance

It rains. A lot. Summer brings spectacular runs of clear days and also entirely grey ones; in winter rain is the default and the volcano appears snow-covered between clouds. A camper changes that equation completely: with a diesel heater, a rainy day stops being a problem and becomes an afternoon of reading with a fogged window and the lake outside.

The only weather advice I give seriously: don't build an itinerary that depends on one specific sunny day. Keep the big-view outings as floating options, and spend the grey days on forest, hot springs and towns.

If a week leaves you wanting more — it usually does — the natural next step is heading down the Carretera Austral. And if you're still choosing dates, read the best time to travel by camper first.

Frequently asked

Can you rent a camper in Puerto Varas?

Yes — it's our base, and pickup here is free. We also deliver to Puerto Montt airport (PMC) for CLP 35,000, 20 minutes away.

What can you do around Puerto Varas with only a few days?

With three days: Osorno volcano, the Petrohué falls and a night in Ensenada, plus the Puerto Varas waterfront and Frutillar. With five to seven days, add Cochamó and Chiloé.

Is Chiloé worth it by camper?

Very much. The ferry from Pargua is short and frequent, needs no booking most of the year, and the island has seafront campsites, UNESCO wooden churches and a food culture distinct from the mainland.

What's the best month to visit Puerto Varas?

January and February for stable weather and crowds; November, March and April for my favourite mix of decent weather, colour and quiet. Winter is wet, but a snow-covered volcano makes up for it.
